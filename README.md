## 🚀 What Is desktop-fly?

desktop-fly is a fun, mesmerizing desktop companion that brings a realistic 3D fruit fly right onto your macOS desktop. This isn't just a simple animated image — the fly's movements are driven by a live simulation of the actual brain wiring (connectome) of a real fruit fly, taken from the scientific FlyWire project. The result is a tiny digital creature that behaves, twitches, and explores in a way that mimics real insect behavior, all while sitting harmlessly on your screen.

Imagine having a tiny, curious pet that lives on your desktop, moves around, and occasionally pauses as if it's thinking. That's exactly what desktop-fly offers.

## ✨ Key Features

- **Realistic 3D Fruit Fly Model** – The fly is rendered in full 3D with detailed wings, body, and eyes, making it look surprisingly lifelike on your screen.

- **Live Spiking Brain Simulation** – Every move the fly makes is computed in real-time using a simplified model of the fruit fly's actual neural circuitry from the FlyWire connectome project. This means no two moments are the same — the fly's behavior is genuinely dynamic.

- **Seamless Desktop Integration** – The fly lives directly on your desktop, above your wallpaper but below your open windows. You can continue working while your tiny companion walks around.

- **Click to Interact** – Click on the fly to make it react, jump, or change direction. It notices your presence.

- **Low Resource Usage** – Despite the complex simulation under the hood, desktop-fly is optimized to run smoothly without draining your battery or slowing down your computer.

- **Automatic Start** – Once installed, desktop-fly can be set to launch automatically when you log in, so your fly is always ready to greet you.

- **Customizable Appearance** – Adjust the fly's size, speed, and color (within natural fruit fly colors) to match your preference.

- **No Annoying Pop-ups or Notifications** – The fly quietly does its thing. You won't get alerts, banners, or sound notifications.

## 🛠️ Troubleshooting

**The fly doesn't appear after launching the app.**

Make sure you've moved the app to your Applications folder and opened it from there. If it still doesn't appear, check your menu bar — there should be a small fruit fly icon. Click it and select "Show/Hide Desktop Fly." Also, make sure your desktop isn't in "Show Desktop" overview mode (the fly will hide during that).

**The fly is moving too fast or too slow.**

Right-click on the fly and adjust the "Speed" slider in the menu. You can also use the "Size" slider to make it larger or smaller.

**I see a warning when opening the app for the first time.**

This is normal for apps downloaded from the internet. Right-click the app and select "Open," then click "Open" in the pop-up window. You only need to do this once.

**The fly disappeared behind my windows.**

That's the intended behavior. The fly stays on your desktop, not above your apps. To see it, minimize all windows or use the "Show Desktop" gesture (swipe down with three fingers, or press F11 if you use a keyboard).

**The app doesn't launch at all.**

Check that your macOS version is 11.0 or later. If it still doesn't work, restart your computer and try again. Make sure you have at least 150 MB of free space.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Is desktop-fly safe to use?**
Absolutely. The app runs entirely on your computer, does not collect any personal data, and does not connect to the internet. It's simply a simulation running locally.

**Will it interfere with my work?**
No. The fly sits behind your active windows and does not capture keyboard input or interfere with clicks meant for other applications. You can work normally while the fly roams your desktop.

**Can I run multiple flies at once?**
A single fly is supported, but you can relaunch the app to reset it. Each launch creates one new fly.

**Does it use a lot of battery power?**
The simulation is lightweight and designed to be efficient. Background resource usage is minimal, comparable to a small widget.

**Will the fly ever "die" or stop working?**
No — the simulation runs indefinitely. You can quit the app anytime from the menu bar icon.
